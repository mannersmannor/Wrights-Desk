# Wrights-Desk
This is your destination for all things Wrights Desk.

Within, find the skeleton plan for a Wright's Desk. All you need to do is print it out, say at a library with a large format printer, a university, or a printing store. Then, modify it how you like by drawing on it. I like to add decorative scrolls near the bottom edge.

Then, cut it out, being careful to only cut out your added custom details on one side, and following the printed line on the other. Then, fold it in half, making sure all of the edges line up. Then, cut your details from the primary side to the mirrored side.

Now, carefully glue or tape this pattern to a piece of cardboard. If you are going to make multiple, be sure to keep this cardboard cutout as your template to trace multiple desks from. If you are only making one, it might make sense to just cut the pattern into your cardboard and fold that into your desk. Up to you!

Lastly, you can mark, cut, and score the bend lines with a straight edge. This may provide better results, but also may weaken it if further steps are not taken. Use a straight-edge. All of the lines should be obvious where they go, saving the fourth line which is on the upper middle section. Simply measure the edge which it will fold to be attached to and add about a millimeter for the length of the bend.

Then fold and tape or glue. I recommend hot glue.

As a final touch, before further finishing, if desired, you can customize the angles. The skeleton plan has equal-length legs. This means that all the display and easel positions are at 45 degrees. Kind of a waste, I know, but this is your desk. I don't want to set you up with an angle you don't like. So all you have to do it pick your favorite side that you are going to use the most. Then set it on the edge of a table and drop the bottom of your favorite down below the edge of the table until you like the angle. Mark your line using the edge of the table, ideally without marking the table (avoid sharpie), and cut it straight. This way, at least one angle of the desk is exactly where you want it, while the other side changes to provide more options. I like to cut 5/8 of an inch off of the outer piece, but I've also been known to leave it the way it is. Just a preferences game.

If you want a more durable upgrade, especially if you pre-scored the bend lines, you can use paper mache. Just pre-cut your paper so it doesn't bunch up and ruin the fit between the two pieces, especially on the outside of the inside piece and the inside of the outside piece. Then you can finish with say, a sealant like an epoxy spray. Also, I hear some paints are waterproof, so you may be able to customize and waterproof in one step.

Another weatherproofing/ strengthening technique is to do paper mache, but with wood glue. This is a two step process, since you are going to want to do the inside of both, then the outside of both. Also, this technique is much more dangerous in terms of potential damage, so be sure to have an adequate space covered in an area where permanent glue spots will not be a problem, like a shed, or over an adequately large tarp, which you don't leave until everything is complete. You will also need gloves and what I call "paint pants" which exist only to get dirty.

Oh, and one more thing. Have fun and enjoy your new art studio that fits in a backpack. Try putting pencil cases, notebooks, rulers, and anything else inside before you put it in your backpack or laptop bag. Another upgrade you can make is make or use and old strap from a bag and some holes in the sides to make either a backpack or a shoulder bag. There are many ways to do it, so don't be afraid to experiment and see what works for you.

That's all for now.
